Most Portuguese in model training data is Brazilian, so default instincts drift to pt-BR. Think in pt-PT from the first word; never draft in pt-BR or English and convert. One variety, one spelling norm and one form of address per text.

## Register

Decide before writing, then hold it for the whole site or brand.

- **Polite, no pronoun (default)**: third-person verb, no subject. "Pode consultar os horários." "Receberá um email de confirmação." Possessive *seu/sua*, object *o/a/lhe*, stressed *si/consigo*: "Isto é para si", "Falamos consigo". Use for businesses, public bodies, services, anything unsure.
- **o senhor / a senhora**: direct formal letters or replies to a named person.
- **tu**: only when the brand chooses it (youth, lifestyle, some social accounts): "Descobre", "Junta-te a nós". Full second-person forms: *tu estás, tu foste*, never *tu foi*.
- **Never *você*** to address the reader: in Portugal it reads distant or rude. Never *a gente* for *nós*.
- Plural *vocês* takes a third-person plural verb, with object *vos* and possessive *vosso*: "Enviamos-vos o programa."
- Buttons and labels use the infinitive, which avoids the choice: "Guardar", "Enviar pedido", "Ver mais".
- Avoid gendered greetings when the reader is unknown: "Boas-vindas" or "Damos-lhe as boas-vindas" rather than "Bem-vindo/a". No *todes*, *x* or *@* endings.

## Grammar

- **Progressive is *estar a* + infinitive**: "está a ser analisado", "estamos a preparar", "A enviar…". Never *estou fazendo*. Same for *andar a, continuar a, ficar a*.
- **Ênclise is the default**: the pronoun follows the verb with a hyphen. "Inscreva-se", "Contacte-nos", "Verifica-se que". Never open a sentence with an unstressed pronoun (*Me disseram*).
- **Próclise when a trigger precedes the verb**: negation (*não, nunca, ninguém, nada*), adverbs such as *já, ainda, também, só, sempre, talvez, bem*, subordinators and relatives (*que, se, quando, porque, como, embora, onde*), question words, and quantifier subjects (*todos, alguém, tudo*). "Não se aplica", "Já lhe enviámos", "o documento que nos pediu". A noun or *eu* as subject is not a trigger: "A equipa contactou-o."
- **Mesóclise** with the synthetic future and conditional: "enviar-lhe-emos", "far-se-á". It is correct but stiff; in copy prefer "vamos enviar-lhe" or the present. Never *enviaremos-lhe* or *Lhe enviaremos*.
- Pronoun shapes: *-lo/-la* after -r, -s, -z ("pode fazê-lo", "vamos enviá-la"), *-no/-na* after nasal endings ("dão-no"); *-mos* before *nos* loses the s ("sentamo-nos"). Objects are never *ele/ela*: "vi-o", not *vi ele*.
- *Haver* for existence, always singular: "Há três vagas", "Houve alterações". Not *tem*, not *haviam*.
- *Ter de* + infinitive; *precisar de* keeps the *de*. *Ir a/para*, *chegar a*, *assistir a*: "Vá ao balcão", "chegar a casa".
- Article before possessives: "o seu pedido", "a nossa equipa".
- 1st person plural past of -ar verbs takes an accent: "enviámos" (past) vs "enviamos" (present).
- *Porque* asks and answers ("Porque é que…?"), *porquê* at the end or as a noun. Not *por que* for "why".
- Personal infinitive is idiomatic: "Obrigado por terem participado", "antes de sair**mos**".

## Spelling (AO1990 as applied in Portugal)

- Silent consonants drop: *ação, ótimo, direção, projeto, atual, receção, aspeto, exceção, detetar*. Pronounced ones stay: *facto, contacto, secção, pacto*. *fato* means a suit.
- Acute, not the Brazilian circumflex: *económico, género, prémio, anónimo, António, eletrónico, ténis, bebé*.
- pt-PT forms: *registo, húmido, dezasseis, dezassete, dezanove, connosco, equipa, câmara, controlo, desporto*.
- No accent on *para, pelo, polo, veem, leem, deem, voo*. *hei de, há de* without hyphen; *fim de semana, dia a dia* without hyphens.
- Lowercase months, weekdays and seasons: "segunda-feira, 3 de março".
- Where two spellings are valid (*setor/sector, caraterística/característica*), pick one per project.
- Pre-1990 spelling (*acção, óptimo*) only when the client's own material uses it consistently. Never mix norms.

## Numbers, dates, money

- Decimal comma, thousands with a space or dot: *3,5*, *1 250* or *1.250*.
- Euro after the amount: **12,50 €**, "a partir de 9,99 €", "IVA incluído".
- 10⁹ is *mil milhões*; *bilião* is 10¹². "$3 billion" is "3 mil milhões de dólares".
- Dates 15/10/2026 or "15 de outubro de 2026"; never month first.
- 24-hour time: *14h30* or *14:30*; ranges written out: "das 9h às 18h".
- Ordinals with a dot: *1.º, 2.ª, n.º*. Phones *+351 912 345 678*, postcodes *1100-148 Lisboa*.
- Percent: *15 %* or *15%*, one style per site.

## Punctuation and headings

- Quotes « », nested " ". If a site already uses " " throughout, keep it.
- No em dash or en dash in running text: use a comma, colon or full stop. Hyphens stay where the language needs them (*verifica-se*, *pequeno-almoço*).
- No comma before *e* in a list; no space before ? ! : ;
- Sentence case for headings, buttons, menus and SEO titles: "Serviços ao munícipe", not "Serviços Ao Munícipe".

## Copy that reads as written in Portugal

- Restrained tone: few exclamation marks, no superlatives, no brochure voice.
- Cut filler: *excelência, tecnologia de ponta, soluções personalizadas, experiência única, ao seu serviço, compromisso com, desempenha um papel crucial, no mundo atual, é importante referir que, vale a pena destacar, mergulhar em, em suma*. *Além disso* is fine once, not as a habit.
- Replace a promise with a fact: a day, an hour, a place, a price, a number of working days.
- Three abstract nouns in a row (*rigor, proximidade e dedicação*) is padding.
- Translating from English: adjective after the noun, unpack noun stacks with prepositions, convert Title Case, drop calques (*eventualmente, realizar* for "realise", *suportar* for "support", *aplicar para*, *endereçar* a problem, *mandatório, providenciar, baseado em*).
- One term per concept. If the project has a style guide or terminology table, it wins; otherwise pick one word and reuse it everywhere.
- Leave verbatim: quotations, legal citations, official names, literal third-party interface labels.

## Review checklist

1. Register chosen and consistent; no *você*, no *a gente*, no stray *tu* in polite copy.
2. No gerund progressive; clitics placed by the rules above; no sentence-initial pronoun.
3. No pt-BR vocabulary or spelling; one spelling norm.
4. Numbers, dates, money and ordinals in pt-PT format.
5. Quotes « »; no em or en dashes in prose; sentence case headings.
6. No filler, no calques, concrete facts; project terms used exactly.
7. Read aloud: would someone at a Portuguese front desk say this?
